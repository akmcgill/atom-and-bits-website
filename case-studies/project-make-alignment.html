Kroger · Manufacturing · Project MAKE

Unsticking a stalled $100M transformation

Two months into an ERP modernization spanning 32 manufacturing facilities, the program was stuck in circles — competing priorities, undefined scope, no agreement on who owned what data. A weeklong alignment workshop turned that stalemate into the plan that carried the program to go-live.

The problem

Project MAKE was a $100M+ effort to move 32 grocery, dairy, and bakery manufacturing facilities onto a modern, data- and AI-enabled operating model — with no inherited documentation, process, or roadmap to build from. Two months in, the program was stuck. The room spanned systems integrators, contract engineers, business stakeholders and SMEs, Kroger product, design, and engineering, plus executives who wanted firm dates. Priorities were competing across every user group, the scope of technical development was still undefined, and nobody had agreed on who owned what data.

Two months of iteration in, there still wasn't a cohesive plan for delivering the ERP to the first plant. Meetings kept surfacing the same disagreements without resolving them — the program had all the ingredients of a transformation and none of the alignment to move it forward.

⚠️
Why this stalled

No single artifact existed yet that every function — engineering, SMEs, systems integrators, and executives — had actually agreed to. Everyone was solving a slightly different version of the problem.

What I did

I led a weeklong alignment workshop built to force the decisions everyone had been avoiding. Rather than another status meeting, the goal was to put every open question on the table at once, in a shared visual format nobody could talk around.

1

End-to-end service blueprint

Mapped every critical workpath across the transformation, identifying user groups, data owners, and exactly where data migration would break if left unresolved.

2

RAID log

Surfaced risks, assumptions, issues, and dependencies that had been discussed informally for weeks but never written down where the whole room could see them together.

3

Roadmap & milestone Gantt chart

Tied the blueprint and RAID log to real dates — sequencing, dependencies, and the milestones the business needed to hit, made concrete instead of aspirational.

What's a RAID log?

Risks, Assumptions, Issues, and Dependencies — a single running document that keeps everything that could derail a program visible in one place, instead of scattered across meeting notes.

Laying all three artifacts out together made the trade-offs impossible to avoid: which workflows had to be sequenced first, whose data-ownership questions were blocking engineering, where scope had to flex to hit a date that mattered to the business. The room stopped debating in the abstract and started making calls.

“
This was the day the project really started. We've just been talking in circles. Now we're on the same page.
— Project sponsor, at the close of the alignment workshop

Outcome

That workshop became the catalyst for the rest of the program — the blueprint, roadmap, and RAID log built that week were the foundation everything downstream got built on. With the plan finally shared and concrete, the team that carried it forward through testing, training, and go-live delivered the rollout with zero plant downtime.
