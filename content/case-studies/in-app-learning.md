# The problem
The objectives were clear: reduce the cost of new-associate onboarding and cut help-desk tickets tied to training
gaps. What was ambiguous was everything underneath that. New retail associates had one day to learn to manage
inventory on an Android handheld device. Training time was limited and often incomplete. Roughly 28% of help-desk
tickets for that device and app traced back to issues basic training should have already covered — but nobody
had defined where the real failure point was, or what to build to fix it.

> CALLOUT (teal): Finding the real failure point
> Analyzed help-desk tickets with Copilot to surface themes and sentiment, then interviewed new associates,
> training managers, and the help-desk team — and did floor-level UX research watching associates actually use
> the device, instead of guessing where an intervention would matter.

# What I did
That research shaped the metric: associates needed microtraining delivered at the moment they hit a problem, not
before, paired with a measurable drop in tickets. It also ruled out the obvious first answer — revising onboarding
curriculum — because interviews showed in-person training wasn't reliably delivered store to store. An embedded,
in-app layer was the only approach that could guarantee the same training quality regardless of which store or
trainer someone got.

## Artifact 1: Ticket analysis
Used Copilot to surface themes and sentiment across help-desk tickets, establishing the 28% baseline tied to
training gaps rather than guessing at the scale of the problem.

## Artifact 2: Interviews
Talked to new associates, training managers, and the help-desk team to understand where training was breaking
down and why in-person delivery varied so widely.

## Artifact 3: Floor-level UX research
Watched associates actually use the device on the floor, to find where a training or help intervention would
matter most instead of guessing.

> CALLOUT (orange): Why in-app, not onboarding
> In-person training wasn't guaranteed and varied significantly store to store. Improving a curriculum that
> wasn't reliably delivered in the first place wouldn't have solved the problem — the training had to live
> inside the workflow itself.

The team prototyped an in-app training and support layer and tested it onsite with store associates before
building further — validating the approach against real behavior on the floor rather than a conference-room
assumption.

# Pull quote
> Formal training was removed from onboarding entirely — the in-app layer wasn't a supplement to what wasn't
> working. It replaced it.
— Karina, on the outcome of the platform's first version

# Outcome
The first version reached 300,000 retail associates and third-party vendors. Formal training was removed from
onboarding entirely, and help-desk tickets dropped 25% in the first quarter. The result also changed how the
business saw in-app learning: it led to a follow-on pilot of Axonify, a third-party AI-driven microlearning
platform, which Karina also owned.
