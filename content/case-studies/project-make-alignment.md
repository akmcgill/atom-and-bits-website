# The problem
Project MAKE was a $100M+ effort to move 32 grocery, dairy, and bakery manufacturing facilities onto a modern,
data- and AI-enabled operating model — with no inherited documentation, process, or roadmap to build from. Two
months in, the program was stuck. The room spanned systems integrators, contract engineers, business stakeholders
and SMEs, Kroger product, design, and engineering, plus executives who wanted firm dates. Priorities were
competing across every user group, the scope of technical development was still undefined, and nobody had agreed
on who owned what data.

Two months of iteration in, there still wasn't a cohesive plan for delivering the ERP to the first plant. Meetings
kept surfacing the same disagreements without resolving them — the program had all the ingredients of a
transformation and none of the alignment to move it forward.

> CALLOUT (teal): Why this stalled
> No single artifact existed yet that every function — engineering, SMEs, systems integrators, and executives —
> had actually agreed to. Everyone was solving a slightly different version of the problem.

# What I did
I led a weeklong alignment workshop built to force the decisions everyone had been avoiding. Rather than another
status meeting, the goal was to put every open question on the table at once, in a shared visual format nobody
could talk around.

## Artifact 1: End-to-end service blueprint
Mapped every critical workpath across the transformation, identifying user groups, data owners, and exactly
where data migration would break if left unresolved.

## Artifact 2: RAID log
Surfaced risks, assumptions, issues, and dependencies that had been discussed informally for weeks but never
written down where the whole room could see them together.

## Artifact 3: Roadmap & milestone Gantt chart
Tied the blueprint and RAID log to real dates — sequencing, dependencies, and the milestones the business needed
to hit, made concrete instead of aspirational.

Laying all three artifacts out together made the trade-offs impossible to avoid: which workflows had to be
sequenced first, whose data-ownership questions were blocking engineering, where scope had to flex to hit a date
that mattered to the business. The room stopped debating in the abstract and started making calls.

# Outcome
That workshop became the catalyst for the rest of the program — the blueprint, roadmap, and RAID log built that
week were the foundation everything downstream got built on. With the plan finally shared and concrete, the team
that carried it forward through testing, training, and go-live delivered the rollout with **zero plant downtime**.
