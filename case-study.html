Internal pilot

Kitchen ERP as a discovery accelerant

Enterprise ERP discovery usually leans on round after round of stakeholder interviews and document reviews before the real dependencies show up — data quality issues, exception handling, permissions, mobility constraints. Too often those surface late, during testing or after launch, once they're expensive to fix.

So we tested a different approach on a real, working system: a household kitchen inventory tool, self-hosted on the open-source platform Grocy, used as a lightweight stand-in for enterprise ERP discovery. The question wasn't really about the kitchen — it was whether building something instead of just talking about it would surface the same kinds of decisions and trade-offs that show up in large-scale ERP projects, and whether it would get there faster.

What building (instead of interviewing) revealed

Real trade-offs surfaced in days, not workshop cycles

Working with a live system instead of a requirements doc forced real decisions out early — the kind that normally hide until testing or launch, once they're expensive to fix. It also gave us a faster, more honest read on where a discovery process needs to flex.

Honest limitations

What this pilot doesn't prove yet

A two-person household is a much simpler stakeholder environment than a multi-department enterprise rollout, where competing incentives — not just missing information — are what actually slow discovery down. We'd want to test this against a situation with real stakeholder conflict before claiming it generalizes to enterprise clients.

What we can say: building a real, working, low-fidelity analog surfaced meaningful trade-offs in days instead of months. That's part of why our discovery process with clients looks the way it does.
