We don't have a published client case study live yet — engagements are underway, and results will land here as they wrap. In the meantime, here's a real pilot that shaped how we approach discovery: proof of method, not a client story.

Internal pilot

Kitchen ERP as a discovery accelerant

Enterprise ERP discovery usually leans on round after round of stakeholder interviews and document reviews before the real dependencies show up — data quality issues, exception handling, permissions, mobility constraints. Too often those surface late, during testing or after launch, once they're expensive to fix.

So we tested a different approach on a real, working system: a household kitchen inventory tool, self-hosted on the open-source platform Grocy, used as a lightweight stand-in for enterprise ERP discovery. The question wasn't really about the kitchen — it was whether building something instead of just talking about it would surface the same kinds of decisions and trade-offs that show up in large-scale ERP projects, and whether it would get there faster.

What building (instead of interviewing) revealed

The same categories that show up in enterprise ERP discovery, just faster

Item / master data setup

Real barcode coverage gaps showed up the moment items got scanned — not from asking "will barcodes work for you?" in an interview.

Inventory zones

The need for unlimited custom storage zones (not a fixed template) was obvious on day one of physically stocking the kitchen — the kind of requirement that's easy to underscope in a workshop.

Barcode / lookup integration

Exposed the unhappy path immediately: what happens when a scan comes back empty. That failure mode often gets glossed over until real testing happens.

Inventory rules

Forced an actual threshold number instead of a vague "let us know when we're running low."

Role & permission model

Landing on a real decision took one round of questions, versus the many workshop cycles a multi-role enterprise permission matrix usually eats up.

Mobility UX

Testing on a real device turned up a genuine compatibility gap within the first two weeks — the kind of finding that often doesn't surface until formal testing, late in an enterprise rollout.

Honest limitations

What this pilot doesn't prove yet

A two-person household is a much simpler stakeholder environment than a multi-department enterprise rollout, where competing incentives — not just missing information — are what actually slow discovery down. This needs to be tested against a situation with real stakeholder conflict before we'd claim it generalizes to enterprise clients. Unit-of-measure configuration also wasn't stress-tested in v1 — that's a known open gap, not a finding.

What we can say: pairing traditional interview-heavy discovery with a real, working, low-fidelity analog surfaced the same kinds of dependencies and trade-offs in days instead of months. That's directly useful for how we run Operational Readiness & Workflow Intelligence Assessments, and it's part of why our discovery process looks the way it does.
